A quantum computer, in contrast to a classical computer, operates based on the principles of quantum mechanics. These machines are especially promising for solving certain types of problems that even supercomputers struggle to address. However, current quantum computers are still in their early stages of development, akin to the vacuum tube era of classical computing. My research focuses mainly on developing and deploying algorithms especially for these Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) machines. I hope all these efforts pave the way to better computing and applications with the advent of better quantum machines in the future! 

I use quantum computing to study quantum many-body systems, which involves preparing quantum states on quantum computers and computing key properties through appropriate measurements. I have used quantum computing to prepare ground states, excited states, and thermal states using various state preparation methods such as adiabatic state preparation, thermofield double state preparation, subspace diagonalization, and linear combination of unitaries. After appropriate state preparation, I have computed many relevant properties such as correlation functions, partition function zeros, and phase transitions using quantum computing. To enhance the accuracy of data obtained from NISQ devices, I employ various error mitigation techniques. 

 Additionally, I have worked on using entanglement witnesses of graph states for benchmarking quantum computers. I also have used machine learning to study quantum computing data.
